The bottleneck

The hazard side got good.
The damage side did not.

A flood model can now resolve inundation to a few metres. The step that turns that water into money is still, in most portfolios, a single curve mapping one depth to one expected damage ratio — and the observations say that curve is not what happens.

Fails quietly

Depth-only transfer

A curve fitted on one country’s building stock, moved to another, carries its construction, code era and repair economics with it — invisibly.

Fails quietly

Payout learned as physics

Calibrating on settled claims without normalising deductibles and limits teaches the model your policy terms and calls the result vulnerability.

Fails quietly

Silent extrapolation

Out-of-domain depth, occupancy or geography returns a number with the same confidence as an in-sample one, and nothing on the screen says so.

    Global-to-local calibration

    The differentiator is not another curve.
    It is knowing what to do with ten observations.

    Local data update the global prior in proportion to their quality and sample size. Ten records shrink hard toward the prior; a thousand records own the answer. The prior is never discarded, so a location with no evidence at all still returns a documented number — with honest width.

    Where claims are scarce

    We elicit distributions, not curve edits — the proportion of masonry in the local stock, typical first-floor height, basement prevalence, code era, repair practice. Expert statements are stored as auditable priors with source, date and confidence, and they move the answer by exactly as much as they should.

    Insurance workflows

    Five desks, one vulnerability view.

    The same versioned engine answers the underwriter, the pricing actuary, the accumulation team, the model-validation function and the claims unit — which is the point. Today those five desks usually disagree because they are quietly running different vulnerability assumptions.

    Two risks, one hazard score, different answers

    Both properties sit in the same 1-in-100 depth band, and a depth-only curve prices them identically. The archetype split is where the underwriting decision actually lives: the same 0.9 m of water is a manageable claim in one and a near-total loss in the other.

    What the underwriter sees

    Not a score. A severity distribution, the archetype it was drawn from, the evidence grade behind it, and a warning if the depth sits outside the calibrated domain.

    Technical price from the whole distribution

    Expected loss comes off the mean; the risk load comes off the tail. A point estimate gives you the first and forces you to guess the second, usually through a flat regional factor that is wrong in both directions at once.

    Because ground-up damage is kept strictly separate from policy terms, the deductible and limit apply downstream of the vulnerability layer — so a change in attachment does not silently rewrite the physics.

    One consistent view across a multinational book

    Each country arrives with its own national classification and its own local functions. The taxonomy crosswalk makes the books comparable; the evidence grade stops that comparability from being mistaken for equal confidence.

    Banks — physical climate risk

    A mortgage book is a portfolio
    of building vulnerabilities.

    Banks are asked to quantify physical climate risk on collateral they did not underwrite for flood, using hazard layers that stop at the water’s edge. The missing step is the same one insurers are missing: what a given depth does to a given building, with the uncertainty stated. Damage on collateral is what moves valuation, LTV, loss given default and, ultimately, expected credit loss.

    Vulnerability is the only link in this chain that is not already in the bank’s stack — and it is the one that decides the size of everything downstream.
    Regulatory

    Stress testing and ICAAP

    Hold vulnerability fixed and move the hazard, or evolve adaptation and hold the hazard — the two are separable, so a supervisor can see which assumption is doing the work.

    Accounting

    IFRS 9 and collateral valuation

    Damage distributions feed a forward-looking haircut on collateral value, and therefore LGD, with provenance a model-validation function can trace back to source evidence.

    Origination

    Lending policy and green finance

    Quantified avoided loss from elevation, floodproofing or utility relocation turns a resilience retrofit into a priceable adjustment rather than a marketing claim.

    Why banks and insurers should use the same layer

    When the lender and the insurer of the same building disagree about its flood vulnerability, one of them is mispricing and neither can prove which. A shared, versioned, independently validated vulnerability layer makes that disagreement visible and testable — which is a precondition for insurance-linked lending terms, resilience-linked mortgages and any credible transfer of physical risk between the two balance sheets.

    Validation and governance

    We would rather be checkable than impressive.

    A vulnerability layer that cannot be independently tested is worth nothing to a model-risk function, whatever it scores. The benchmark programme is pre-specified, reproducible and open to review — and the marketing language is held to what it has actually passed.

    Coverage is global. Confidence is not.

    The API returns a scientifically documented answer anywhere on earth. What changes by geography is the evidence grade attached to it — and we would rather show you a D than dress it up as an A.
